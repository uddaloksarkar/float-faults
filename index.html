01 / What changes

The mean looks fine. The spread does not.

For a Poisson distribution with rate λ, both the mean and variance equal λ. That gives us a direct check: draw many samples and divide their empirical variance by λ. The result should be close to 1.

02 / Why it happens

Rounding error compromises the sampler’s internal decision.

At large λ, NumPy proposes a count k from a broader distribution, then uses an acceptance test to trim the tails back to a Poisson distribution. Some proposals take a fast accept path. For the others, the test needs the Poisson log probability of k.

That log probability changes by only about 0.5 between a central count and one standard deviation into the tail. But NumPy computes it by subtracting terms near 10²⁰. Rounding can erase the small difference, so the test no longer rejects tail proposals at the intended rate. The measured output is therefore too wide.

The cancellation in the acceptance testlog P(K = k) = −λ + k log(λ) − log Γ(k + 1)

At λ = 9 × 10¹⁸, compare a count at the center with two counts in the upper tail:

CandidateIdeal log probabilityIllustrative binary64 result
k = λ−22.740
k = λ + √λ−23.240
k = λ + 2√λ−24.740

The ideal score falls as k enters the tail; the rounded illustration gives all three candidates the same score. A rejection test using such scores cannot apply the needed tail correction. Here k log(λ) ≈ 3.93 × 10²⁰, where adjacent binary64 numbers are 65,536 apart.

What this project covers
Start with NumPy. Then examine the wider ecosystem.

        
This catalog starts with NumPy because its implementations and runnable examples make the faults concrete. Other libraries may use different algorithms and thresholds, so each one needs its own measurement and source-level analysis. The broader research goal is to quantify how far implemented samplers can depart from the distributions that applications assume.
