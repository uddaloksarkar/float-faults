01 / What changes

The mean looks fine. The spread does not.

For a Poisson distribution with rate λ, both the mean and variance equal λ. That gives us a direct check: draw many samples and divide their empirical variance by λ. The result should be close to 1.

02 / Why it happens

Rounding moves the acceptance boundary.

For λ ≥ 10, NumPy uses transformed rejection: propose an integer k, accept some proposals on a fast path, and test the rest against the Poisson log mass. The distribution depends on that last comparison being resolved accurately.

The issue is specific to the evaluated program. Rounding in log(λ), the product, NumPy’s log-gamma routine, and the subtractions all enter the decision; rearranging an expression that is equal over the reals can change the sampler’s output distribution.

Poisson side of the accept/reject test−λ + k log(λ) − log Γ(k + 1)

For the live example, λ = 9 × 10¹⁸ and k ≈ λ:

Large intermediatek log(λ) ≈ 3.93 × 10²⁰
Binary64 spacing there1 ulp = 65,536
Ideal log mass near the mode≈ −22.74

At k = λ + t√λ, the exact log mass changes by roughly −t²/2: an order-one signal across the natural width of the distribution. A one-ulp perturbation of the large product is therefore vastly bigger than the variation the test must distinguish. After cancellation, the rounded score can place tail proposals on the wrong side of the acceptance boundary.

This scale comparison explains the numerical vulnerability; the measured variance inflation is empirical. The full sampler also rounds its proposal and has a fast accept branch. The manuscript analyzes error through the exact operation sequence rather than treating the formula alone as a proof of the observed ratio.

What this project covers
Start with NumPy. Then examine the wider ecosystem.

        
This catalog starts with NumPy because its implementations and runnable examples make the faults concrete. Other libraries may use different algorithms and thresholds, so each one needs its own measurement and source-level analysis. The broader research goal is to quantify how far implemented samplers can depart from the distributions that applications assume.
